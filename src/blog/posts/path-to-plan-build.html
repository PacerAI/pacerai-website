What is the path to plan?

Will Sullivan November 14, 2026

Path to plan is booked ARR compared with the plan prorated to today. It tells a CRO whether the company is ahead of the annual intention, behind it, or about to be. Pacer AI is a bottom-up sales forecasting and planning agent. The path is how you make plan with confidence during the year. The real-time forecast, which adds weighted open pipeline to booked ARR, is the second number, not the first.

What does path to plan measure?

The annual plan is a full-year intention. Path to plan cuts that intention down to the day you are standing on. If the plan is a straight line from opening ARR to the sales target, today’s plan-to-date is the point on that line. Booked ARR is what has actually been added. The difference is the gap.

Illustrative book, the same one on the homepage. The fiscal year takes ARR from $100M to $120M. By September 20 the straight-line plan to date is about $114.4M. Booked ARR of $112.2M means the company is $2.2M behind the plan. These figures are illustrative. They are not a customer result.

Pacer AI reports this for the month, the quarter and the year. The cut matters. A company can be ahead for the month and behind for the year. The year is the plan. The month is the coaching window.

How is the path different from the forecast?

Path to plan uses booked ARR only. It does not give credit for deals that have not closed. The bottom-up forecast adds weighted open pipeline to booked ARR and produces a landing number. Both numbers should be on the same page. If you only watch the landing number, a weak book can hide behind a heavy pipeline. If you only watch bookings, you ignore the pipe that can still close the gap.

The real-time forecast is the landing number updated as the pipe moves. It does not replace the path. It tells you whether the gap is closable.

Where does the path break?

Three places, and each one has an owner.

  1. The baseline. Sales and Finance are not on the same opening ARR. The path is then a debate, not a measurement. Lock it in the bottom-up plan.
  2. The shape of the year. A straight line is the default. A business with a December renewal spike should prorate on the renewal calendar, not on an even twelfth. Say which one you are using.
  3. The cut. A company path can look fine while one market, one segment or a handful of reps are behind. The path has to be readable by market, segment and rep, because that is where the coaching happens. Quota is the rep cut: plan to quota.

What do you do with a gap?

Name the dollars, then name the two motions that can still produce them. Expansion white space at accounts that renew soon, and new-logo prospects, with warm ones first where a past champion now works. The expansion math is in ARR snowball analysis. The product-level gap, what each account does not own yet, is the white space view on that page.

A gap that is only a sentence (“we will make it up”) is not a path. A gap with accounts, products and reps is a plan for the rest of the period.

Which meeting uses which cut?

The right question is which meeting uses which cut. The Monday forecast meeting uses the week and the quarter. The monthly business review uses the month and the year. The board uses the year, with the bridge. CROs and VPs of Sales live in the rep and market cuts. RevOps keeps the definitions. CFOs check that the path still ties to reported ARR.

SaaS Capital publishes the retention ranges private companies actually report, which is the installed-base half of any path. Bessemer’s Atlas is the public-cloud reference for how growth and retention sit together. OpenView and SaaStr both treat the in-year number as an operating rhythm, not a quarterly surprise.

What does a month on the path look like?

Take the illustrative book and stand in it twice.

At the end of January the straight-line plan has moved about one-twelfth of the $20M addition, roughly $1.7M, so plan to date is about $101.7M. Suppose booked ARR is $101.1M. The company is $0.6M behind. That is a small gap, and it may sit in one slipped new logo. The action is specific: which deal, which week, which rep. You do not rewrite the year because January was light, and you do not ignore it because “it is early.”

At September 20 the same ruler says $114.4M and the book is $112.2M. The gap is $2.2M. Nine months of small slips have compounded, or one market has missed. The path by market, segment and rep tells you which. A company total hides a region that is $3M behind and another that is $0.8M ahead. The coaching list is the region, then the reps, then the accounts.

Each month the review has the same three lines, in this order. Where are bookings against the prorated plan. Which cut holds the gap. Which accounts can still close it before the period ends, split into expansion on the installed base and new logos in the pipe. The forecast answers the third line with weights. The path asks it with names.

Materiality keeps the meeting short. In a $100M book, a $50K slip is a note. A $500K slip is an item. Set the line once, with Finance, and hold it. A path review that discusses every deal is a pipeline review that has forgotten its job.

The year-end version of this page is the board bridge. If September is $2.2M behind the path, the board packet should say so in September, with the landing number beside it, not in January when the year is already over. That packet is the board report.

Keep a single prorate rule in writing next to the path. Straight line, renewal-weighted, or quarter targets from the plan: pick one and date it. When someone proposes a new shape because the month looks bad, that is a planning change, and it goes through the same sign-off as the original plan. The path is only a measurement if the ruler stays still between reviews.

Frequently asked questions

What is path to plan?

Booked ARR compared with the annual plan prorated to today. It is the gap between what the plan said you would have by now and what you have booked.

Does path to plan include open pipeline?

No. Open pipeline belongs in the forecast landing number. The path is bookings against the prorated plan, so a heavy pipe cannot disguise a light book.

What if the business is not linear?

Prorate on the calendar you actually sell, such as renewal months, and say so. A straight line is the default when you have not earned a better shape.
