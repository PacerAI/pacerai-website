A top-down plan starts with a growth rate and divides it. A bottom-up plan starts with who will buy, what they will buy, and which rep owns the line. Finance still owns the definitions. The plan has to reconcile to the ARR Finance reports.

Why does the annual plan start at the account?

The board target is one figure. The work that hits it is not. Each existing account has a renewal, an upsell and a cross-sell. Each open opportunity has a product, a close month and an owner. A plan that cannot name those lines cannot be coached, and it cannot be defended when the quarter slips.

Pacer AI starts from the ARR waterfall, the bridge that moves beginning ARR to ending ARR. New, expansion, contraction and churn are the movements. The plan says how much of each movement the company intends to produce, by quarter, and which accounts and reps are supposed to produce it.

How does a bottom-up annual plan get built?

Five steps, in this order.

  1. Lock opening ARR with Finance. If Sales and Finance do not share beginning ARR, every later variance is an argument about the baseline.
  2. List the book. Accounts, products owned, renewal month, and the rep.
  3. Name the motions. New logo, upsell, cross-sell, downsell, churn. Each dollar in the plan sits in one motion.
  4. Assign an owner. A line without a rep is a wish.
  5. Sum it. Account plans sum to quotas. Quotas sum to the sales target. If they do not, the plan is not done.

How does the plan become a sales target and a quota?

The sales target is the ending number the operating plan has to hit. Quota is that target carried to each rep. A plan that is not equal to the sum of quotas will miss, and the miss will not have an owner. The two companion pages are plan to sales target and plan to quota.

Capacity has to be in the plan, not beside it. A rep who starts in March does not carry a full-year quota. A territory with half the accounts of the next territory does not get the same number. Ramp, coverage and the product mix are inputs to the sum. They are not a speech after the target is already announced.

What does the planning cycle actually hand you?

A finished cycle is a set of files, not a narrative. Each one has an owner and a tie-out.

  • Opening ARR sign-off. One balance, Finance and Sales, dated. Everything else is a movement off that balance.
  • The motion bridge for the year. New logo, upsell, cross-sell, downsell, churn, by quarter. The quarters sum to the year. The year nets to the sales target.
  • The account list. Every renewal, every intended expansion, every new logo with a close quarter and a product. Lines with no product are not in the plan.
  • The quota file. Rep, ramp month, quota by motion. The sum matches the bridge.
  • The exceptions. Hiring that has not happened, territories that are open, products that are not shipping. Each exception names the dollars it is holding and the date it has to be resolved.

Quarter shape matters as much as the year total. A plan that puts 40% of new logos in Q4 is a bet on a late pipe. Write the quarters down so the path in April is a comparison with April’s intention, not with a twelfth of a December-heavy year. If renewals cluster, the expansion quarter should cluster with them. A smooth line that ignores the renewal calendar will call a normal January “behind” and a normal October “ahead.”

Two reviews close the cycle. The first is internal: RevOps walks the CRO through every exception over a materiality line you set, for example any line above $100K in the illustrative book. The second is with Finance: the bridge’s ending ARR equals the target, and the opening ARR equals the last reported balance. If either review changes a number, the quota file changes in the same week. A plan that Finance accepts and Sales does not load into quotas is two plans.

During the year the plan is not rebuilt every Monday. It is the ruler. The forecast moves. When a structural fact changes, a product slips or a team is not hired, you re-open the exception list and either replace the dollars or accept a lower target. That decision is a planning decision. It belongs in writing.
