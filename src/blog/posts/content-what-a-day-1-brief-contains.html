A Day 1 brief is the sales review a CRO walks in with on the first day of the month: 13 slides that show how the month that just closed landed against plan, by motion, market, segment and rep, and what carries into the next month. It answers the questions a sales leadership team asks first, in the order they ask them, with every figure tied back to the company total.

This post walks through what is on each slide, the one rule that decides how a dollar figure prints, and the checks that make the numbers safe to forward. The example is the October 2026 deck. Download the October deck as a PDF to follow along, or send it to your RevOps lead.

Whose numbers are in the October deck?

The numbers are the demo book's. October 2026 is a fictional company's month, built on Pacer AI's demo data. The 20% miss, the five large churns, the named reps and the named accounts are all sample data, and none of it belongs to a customer. The deck does not say so on its face, so read it as a worked example of the format and not as a real company's quarter.

The 13 slides, in order

Each entry opens with the slide's headline exactly as it is printed in the deck, then says what the slide answers. Slides 1 to 6 set the result, slides 7 to 10 explain it and look ahead, and slides 11 to 13 are the appendix.

  1. Day 1 Sales Review
    The cover. It names the month and puts the result next to the plan: Net New ARR of $1.53M against a $1.92M plan. It answers the first question in the room: did we make the number?
  2. October: 20% miss ($386K) due to 5 large churn events
    The headline. It answers how big the miss was and where it sits. Three cards split it by motion, with New Logo at −$225K, Net Expansion at −$161K and Net New ARR at −$386K. A table below splits it by segment.
  3. New Logo, Cross-sell and Renewals underperformed
    The ARR bridge. It answers what carried ARR from the start of the month to the end: $117.56M at the start, $1.15M lost, $2.68M added and $119.10M at the end.
  4. A ~$300K increase in churn caused the Net New ARR miss
    The waterfall table. It answers whether October was a one-off or a trend, by setting August, September and October side by side. Each movement also shows as a share of beginning ARR, with monthly gross and net revenue retention underneath.
  5. Every market missed its sales target
    Markets. It answers which geographies fell short of their own plans: the United States booked $950K (83% of plan), Canada $407K (82%) and Europe $173K (63%).
  6. Every segment missed its target too
    Segments. It answers which customer size fell short: Enterprise booked $1.08M (81%), Mid-Market $373K (86%) and SMB $78K (53%), with each segment split by market.
  7. Five large churns ($60K+) offset New Logo and Net Expansion gains
    Top movers. It answers which named accounts moved the number. Four cards list the five biggest accounts each for new logos, churn, upsell and cross-sell.
  8. Team attainment landed at 58% of quota: Target was 65%
    Reps. It answers who carried the month and who is behind. The four reps over quota are listed with what they booked, then come the reps who finished close and the reps who are behind.
  9. Good work.
    The close. It thanks the team, gives the date of the monthly business review (November 16) and sets the ask: bring your November pipe, where the Go-Get, the gap between the pipe and the plan, is $611K and 18 churn risks need defending.
  10. Pipe bridge: what closed in October and what carries into November
    The pipe bridge. It answers what happened to the 255 deals due to close in October (10 new logos won, 32 lost, 190 renewed, 23 churned) and what carries into November: 215 deals, a $611K Go-Get and 18 churn risks.
  11. The $386K miss was driven by the Enterprise segment
    Appendix. It answers where the miss came from when the book is cut by segment, market, customer vintage and tech stack, and ends on a one-line answer: Enterprise accounts in the US, from the 2021 and 2022 vintages.
  12. Enterprise is two-thirds of the miss
    Appendix. Slide 6 turned around: each market's bookings are stacked by segment against that market's plan, so the Enterprise share of each market's bookings is visible. There is no plan for a market and segment together.
  13. ARR by tech stack grew 14–22% over the last four quarters
    Appendix. It answers which CRM and finance-system combinations are growing fastest, from December 2025 to September 2026, with the company as a whole up 18%.

The units rule

Headline figures for the company, markets and segments print one of two ways. At or above $1M, a figure prints in millions to two decimals: October's Net New ARR is $1.53M and the Enterprise segment is $1.08M. Under $1M, a figure prints in thousands with no decimals: the miss is $386K, the Europe market is $173K and the SMB segment is $78K.

Rep quota, rep bookings and single-deal values print in thousands, whatever their size, so a rep's quota reads $183K. ARR levels on the bridge print in millions to two decimals, such as $117.56M and $119.10M, because they are balances and not movements.

The deck departs from the rule in three places, and each has a reason. Chart axes step in round amounts in millions, so an axis can read in millions beside a label in thousands. The waterfall table on slide 4 is in millions throughout, without dollar signs, so that three months line up in one column and a small movement such as downsell reads as a fraction of a million. The summary lines for the full-year forecast on slide 2 ($121.5M against $123.0M, a $1.5M Go-Get) and the pipe on slide 10 (for example $11.7M of current ARR, and $1.3M in the pipe against a $1.9M plan) print to one decimal.

The point of the rule is that, on the headline figures, a reader does not have to work out which unit a figure is in: a miss reads $386K and a month's result reads $1.53M.

The tie-outs, worked on October's numbers

A brief that people forward has to add up. Before a Day 1 brief goes out it checks itself, and if a check fails it stops. These are the checks, with October's numbers. A difference of a thousand dollars between a sum and a total is rounding, not an error.

Markets to company

The three markets add up to the company: $950K + $407K + $173K is the company's $1.53M.

Segments to company

The three segments add up to the same total: $1.08M + $373K + $78K is $1.53M. The plan and the variance tie the same way. The segment variances of −$258K, −$60K and −$69K make up the −$386K miss, and the motion split ties too: New Logo at −$225K plus Net Expansion at −$161K is −$386K.

The bridge to Net New ARR

Everything the bridge adds, less everything it loses, is Net New ARR. The bridge adds +$2.68M (upsell $2.16M, cross-sell $201K and new logo $325K) and loses $1.15M (account churn $1.05M, plus $104K of downsell and product churn). Added +$2.68M less lost $1.15M is $1.53M, the same Net New ARR as the cover. Ending ARR of $119.10M is beginning ARR of $117.56M plus that figure, and because the levels are rounded to two decimals the printed levels can differ from the sum by a hundredth of a million.

Each top-movers card to the rows it shows

Each top-movers card is titled with the company's total for that movement, and its header row shows the sum of the five accounts listed. The new-logo card is titled $325K, and its five rows ($66K, $52K, $50K, $35K and $29K) sum to the $232K in its header. The churn card is titled −$1.05M, and its five rows sum to −$357K. Upsell is titled $2.16M with five rows summing to +$252K, and cross-sell is titled $201K with five rows summing to +$157K. The gap between a card's title and its header is the long tail of smaller deals the card does not list. The slide's headline also holds to the full list: five churns of $60K or more.

Reps to the team figure

Every quota-carrying rep lands in exactly one group: four over quota, one close and 25 behind, which is the 30 quota-carrying account executives. The team figure in the headline, 58% of quota, is total booked divided by total quota. It is not the share of reps who hit quota, which would be 4 of 30.

The pipe bridge

The pipe bridge ties on counts. The 255 deals due to close in October are the 42 new-logo deals plus the 213 renewals. The outcomes add back to the same total: 10 new logos won and 32 lost, 190 renewed and 23 churned.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Day 1 brief?

A Day 1 brief is a 13-slide review of the month that just closed, built on the first day of the next month. It shows Net New ARR against plan by motion, market, segment and rep, explains the biggest movements, and looks ahead to the pipe the team carries into the new month.

Are the numbers in the October deck real?

No. They are the demo book's, which is a fictional company's data. The 20% miss, the reps and the accounts are sample data and are not a customer's.

Why do some figures print in millions and others in thousands?

Headline figures for the company, markets and segments at or above $1M print in millions to two decimals, such as $1.53M, and figures under $1M print in thousands with no decimals, such as $386K. Rep quotas, rep bookings and single-deal values print in thousands. A few places differ, such as chart axes, the waterfall table in millions, and the one-decimal full-year forecast and pipe figures.

How do I know the numbers add up?

The brief checks itself before it goes out. Markets sum to the company ($950K + $407K + $173K is $1.53M), segments sum to the company ($1.08M + $373K + $78K), the bridge's +$2.68M less $1.15M equals the $1.53M of Net New ARR, each top-movers card sums to its rows, and the reps account for every quota-carrying account executive.
