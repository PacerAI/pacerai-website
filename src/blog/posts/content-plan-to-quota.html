Plan to quota carries the company sales plan down to the number each rep has to book. The sum of quotas has to equal the sales target. If it does not, the plan and the commission plan are describing two different companies. Pacer AI is a bottom-up sales forecasting and planning agent for CROs and VPs of Sales. Quota is how the plan becomes a job. Making plan with confidence starts here, before the real-time forecast updates the in-year read.

What is plan to quota?

The bottom-up plan names the dollars by account, product and motion. Quota is the same dollars, grouped by the person who has to bring them in. A rep’s quota is the sum of the lines they own. A manager’s number is the sum of the reps. The CRO’s number is the sum of the managers, and it has to match the sales target.

Illustrative split, not a customer result. The company needs $20M of net new ARR to move from $100M to $120M. Forty quota-carrying reps, if the book were perfectly even, would each carry $500K. Real books are not even. A rep hired in March does not carry $500K. A territory with the enterprise renewals does not carry the same mix as a territory of new logos. The identity still holds: when you are done assigning, the quotas add to $20M.

Why does a single quota number fail?

A rep can hit a blended quota by over-producing new logos and ignoring the installed base, or the reverse. The company plan cared about the mix. The commission plan then pays for a different mix. By the third quarter the path is off and nobody can say whose lines were supposed to carry expansion.

Put the motions inside the quota where the plan depends on them. Expansion white space is a set of accounts and products, not a percent at the bottom of a slide. That view is the cross-sell and upsell white space cut.

How do you know a rep is ahead or behind?

Compare what the rep has booked with their quota prorated to today. That is path to plan at the person. The company can be $2.2M behind, in the illustrative September book, while four reps are ahead and the rest of the gap sits in six territories. The coaching list is those six, with the accounts that can still move.

The landing number for that rep adds their weighted open pipeline to what they have booked. That is the bottom-up forecast at rep grain. Use it to decide whether the gap is in the book or still in the pipe. Do not use it as a substitute for the prorated quota.

Who owns the quota file?

RevOps owns the file. Finance owns the definitions and the tie to the sales target. The VP of Sales owns the assignments. The CRO owns the decision when the sum does not match the target: change the coverage, change the ramp plan, or change the target. Changing the target in January is a planning decision. Changing it in October is a miss.

SaaS Capital shows why the installed base has to be in somebody’s quota: retention is the metric private-company boards ask for. Bessemer and OpenView treat net retention as a company outcome, which means some rep, somewhere, had the expansion in their number. SaaStr’s operating writing comes back to the same test: if the quotas do not add up, the plan is a poster.

What does one manager’s number look like?

Illustrative team, not a customer. A manager owns $2.0M of the company’s $20M net new ARR. Three reps carry it.

Rep B carries more expansion because the territory holds the renewals. Rep A carries more new logo because the territory is thinner on installed ARR. Rep C’s $500K is a three-quarter assignment. The other quarter of that territory, $167K if you were spreading a full-year $667K, has to be named: held by the manager, covered by an overlay, or removed from the company target. Leaving it “in the team number” without an owner is how $167K becomes a surprise.

The weekly check is not a new quota. It is bookings and pipe against this table, prorated. If Rep B is behind on expansion in June, the conversation is the renewal list, not a speech about effort. If Rep C is ahead of a three-quarter pace, you do not silently raise the quota in July. You decide, in writing, whether the assignment changes.

Overlay roles, solution consultants and partners, need the same test. If partner-sourced logos are in the company target, they are in someone’s quota or in an explicit partner line. Dollars that are in the target and in nobody’s quota are the dollars you will miss without a name next to them.

Comp plans follow this table. Pay the motion you planned. If expansion is half of Rep B’s quota and the comp plan pays only on new logos, you planned one year and bought another. Fix the plan or fix the pay. Do not expect a kickoff speech to do it.

Publish the quota file before the year starts, with the ramp dates on it. A rep who learns their number in a kickoff and then sees a different number in the commission system will stop trusting both. The file, the comp plan and the company target are one set. RevOps is responsible for showing they match, in that order: target, then quotas, then the pay formula. If you can only finish two of the three before kickoff, do not finish the speech and skip the tie-out.

Frequently asked questions

What is plan to quota?

It is the company sales plan assigned to reps so that the sum of quotas equals the sales target, with ramp and territory written in.

Should quota match the forecast?

Quota matches the plan. The forecast is the current read of what will land. A rep can be behind quota and still forecast a recovery if the pipe supports it. Those are two different sentences.

What about a rep who starts mid-year?

Their quota is the portion of the year they carry, and the rest of their territory’s annual number has to sit with someone else or with a hiring plan that is explicit.
